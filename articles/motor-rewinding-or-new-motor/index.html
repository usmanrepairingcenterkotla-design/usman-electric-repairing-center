Motor rewinding or a new motor: which is better?

Published: 2026-10-20

When a fan, pump or toka motor burns out, the first question is whether to rewind it or buy a new one. After 20 years of rewinding, here's how we decide.

  1. Rewind when the body is soundIf the motor body, shaft and bearings are in good shape, only the copper winding has failed. Rewinding brings it back to full strength.
  2. Rewind larger and better motorsFor 1 HP motors and above, and for good quality motors of any size, rewinding usually costs much less than a new motor.
  3. Buy new when the body is damagedA cracked body, a bent shaft or a worn bearing seat can't be fixed by rewinding. Replace the motor.
  4. Buy new for very cheap small motorsSome small, low-cost motors cost about the same new as a rewind. We tell you when that's the case.
  5. Watch for poor rewindingThinner wire than the original and missing insulation make a motor run hot and burn again quickly. Ask what gauge of wire is being used.
  6. Fix the cause tooA motor often burns because of a bad capacitor, low voltage or a jammed pump. If the cause isn't fixed, the new winding will burn as well.

Bring the motor in or ask for free home service. We check it first and tell you honestly which option makes sense.
